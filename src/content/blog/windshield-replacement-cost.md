Search "windshield replacement cost" and you'll get numbers that disagree by
hundreds of dollars. They're not all wrong. The price of a windshield can vary
more than almost any other routine car repair, because "a windshield" isn't
one part — it's dozens of different parts that happen to look alike.

This guide covers what actually moves the number, gives the general ranges
published by national cost guides, and explains how to get a real figure for
your own vehicle.

## The short answer: a range, not a price

One independent national cost guide,
[Glass and Auto](https://www.glassandauto.com/cost-guides/windshield-replacement-cost),
puts a typical 2026 windshield replacement at roughly **$250 to $800**, with
an average around $450. Vehicles that need an ADAS camera recalibration
afterwards add to that, and luxury, EV and head-up display windshields can
run well past the top of the range.

Treat those as national ballpark figures. They're not a quote from us or
anyone else, and Orange County pricing can differ. The rest of this guide is
about why your vehicle lands where it does in that range.

## 1. The vehicle, and what's built into its glass

This is the biggest single factor. An older or economy car with no sensors on
the windshield takes what is essentially a sheet of laminated glass, and that
is the cheapest part in the category.

Newer vehicles build features into the glass itself:

- **Rain and light sensors** mounted behind the mirror
- **Heating elements** for the wiper park area or the whole screen
- **Acoustic lamination**, an extra layer to cut wind and road noise
- **Head-up display (HUD) areas**, made to project without a double image
- **Camera and sensor brackets** for lane keeping and automatic braking

Each one changes which windshield fits. The same model and year can take
several different windshields depending on trim, which is why an accurate
number always starts with year, make, model and trim — and often a photo of
the area behind the mirror.

## 2. OEM, OEE or aftermarket glass

There are three broad kinds of replacement glass, and the difference in cost
can be large:

| Glass type | What it is | General cost position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OEM | Made by or for the carmaker, to its specification | Highest |
| OEE | Made to OEM specifications by another manufacturer | Middle |
| Aftermarket | Made to fit the vehicle by an independent manufacturer | Lowest |

The same national guide lists aftermarket glass from around $200 to $300 and
OEM from around $400 to as much as $1,500, depending on the vehicle. All
windshields sold in the US have to meet the same federal safety standard, so
aftermarket isn't automatically unsafe. The honest trade-off is fit and, on
camera-equipped vehicles, how reliably the camera calibrates to the new
glass. It's worth asking which type you're being quoted, and why.

## 3. ADAS recalibration

If your vehicle has a camera mounted behind the mirror for lane keeping,
automatic emergency braking or adaptive cruise control, replacing the
windshield usually means recalibrating that camera afterwards. The camera's
aim depends on the glass it's mounted to, and new glass moves it slightly.

The national guide puts
[calibration](https://www.glassandauto.com/cost-guides/adas-calibration-cost)
at roughly **$150 to $600** on top of the glass. Where you land depends on
the method the carmaker requires: static calibration (targets set up in front
of the parked car), dynamic calibration (a road drive with a scan tool
connected), or both. Our
[ADAS recalibration guide](/blog/adas-recalibration-after-windshield-replacement/)
explains the difference and what to ask for in writing.

A resin chip repair doesn't move the camera, so it never needs
recalibration — one more reason to fix a chip before it becomes a crack.

## 4. Mobile or in-shop

The glass and the labor are the same job wherever it's done. Whether a mobile
visit adds a charge varies from one company to the next, so ask when you get
a quote rather than assuming either way.

What [mobile auto glass service](/mobile-auto-glass/) changes is the cost of
your time: no drop-off, no ride to arrange, no afternoon in a waiting room.
For a work vehicle or a fleet, that's often the part that matters most.

## 5. Whether you need a replacement at all

The cheapest windshield replacement is the one you don't need. A chip smaller
than about a quarter, away from the edge and out of the driver's line of
sight, is usually a resin repair for a fraction of the cost of new glass. The
[repair-or-replace tool](/windshield-repair-or-replace/) walks through it in
about thirty seconds.

## What about insurance?

If you carry comprehensive coverage, a windshield is often covered, subject to

## Getting a real number for your vehicle

A range tells you what's normal. It can't tell you what your windshield will
cost. For that, the quote needs your year, make, model and trim, whether
there's a camera behind the mirror, and ideally a photo of the damage.
