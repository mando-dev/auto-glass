A chip that sits quietly for weeks on one car can run into a foot-long crack
overnight on another. The difference usually isn't luck — it's heat, and
Orange County gives glass plenty of it.

## Why heat is the real enemy of a small chip

A windshield chip is a stress point. The glass around it is under tension,
and anything that expands or contracts the glass unevenly puts more load on
that exact spot. A hot afternoon followed by a cold night does this
naturally. So does climbing into a car that's been sitting in direct sun and
immediately blasting the air conditioning onto the windshield — the glass
right in front of the vents cools faster than the rest of it, and that
temperature difference is often enough to send a stable chip into a running
crack.

Parking in direct sun for a full day, then driving straight into a cold
evening, does something similar more slowly.

## Santa Ana winds and road debris

Santa Ana wind season kicks up dust, grit, and small debris that a normal
day on the freeway wouldn't. Combined with ongoing freeway construction
across the county, loose gravel and small rock chips off passing trucks are
a genuinely common source of fresh damage — not a rare event.

## What actually helps while you wait for a repair

None of this fixes a chip, but it buys you time until it can be looked at:

- **Park in shade when you can.** Less direct heat means less thermal
  stress on the glass.
- **Ease into the air conditioning.** Let the cabin cool gradually for the
  first minute or two rather than pointing full blast straight at the
  windshield.
- **Avoid pressure-washing near the chip.** Water forced into the damage
  under pressure can make it worse, not cleaner.
- **Don't pour hot water on a frosted windshield.** The sudden temperature
  swing is exactly the kind of stress that turns a chip into a crack.

## The don't list, specifically

- **Don't use superglue or a DIY filler kit on a chip you plan to have
  professionally repaired.** It can interfere with a proper resin repair
  later.
- **Don't ignore a chip because it's small.** Small is exactly the size
  that's still repairable — waiting is what turns it into a replacement.
- **Don't drive on a crack that's already crossed the driver's line of
  sight.** At that point it's a visibility and safety issue, not just a
  cosmetic one.
