Windshield damage is one of the more straightforward insurance claims you'll
ever file, but the process still has a few steps worth knowing before you
call — mostly so you don't end up on hold twice.

## Which part of your policy covers it

Glass damage from a rock, a branch, or something you can't identify normally
falls under **comprehensive** coverage, not collision — comprehensive is the
part of a policy that handles damage from something other than a crash with
another vehicle. If your policy only carries liability, glass damage usually
isn't covered at all. Check your declarations page, or just ask your insurer
directly what your policy includes before you assume either way.

## Why a chip repair and a full replacement can be treated differently

Some insurers waive the deductible for a windshield **repair** specifically,
even when the deductible applies to a full **replacement**. The logic is
simple from their side: a resin repair is a small cost that stops a chip from
turning into a claim for an entire windshield later. Not every insurer does
this, and not every state requires it, so it's worth asking your insurer
directly rather than assuming your policy works the same way a friend's does.

## What to have ready before you call

Three things make the call faster on both ends:

1. **Vehicle year, make, model and trim.** Windshields vary more than people
   expect between trim levels — features like rain sensors or a
   forward-facing camera change which glass your insurer needs to authorize.
2. **A photo of the damage.** Size, shape and position all factor into
   whether it's a repair or replacement claim.
3. **Where and roughly when it happened.** Most insurers ask for at least an
   approximate date and location, even for glass claims.

## Choosing who does the work

In most cases, you're entitled to choose your own glass shop rather than
being routed to whichever one your insurer suggests first — check your policy
or ask directly, since this can vary. If you already have a shop you trust,
it's reasonable to ask them directly whether they handle insurance billing,
or whether you pay out of pocket and submit the claim yourself.

## Is it even worth filing a claim for a small chip?

That depends entirely on your deductible relative to what the repair itself
would cost you directly — not something we can answer generically, since it
comes down to your specific policy. If your insurer waives the deductible for
repairs (see above), filing is usually the easy call. If it doesn't, it's
worth comparing the repair cost against your deductible before deciding
whether a claim makes sense at all versus just paying for the repair
directly.

## What to check before booking, so the paperwork is clean

| Ask | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Do you bill insurance directly, or do I pay and submit? | Avoids a surprise about how payment works |
| Can you provide an itemized invoice? | Most insurers want one for reimbursement |
| Does the quote include everything the job needs? | Some jobs involve more than the glass alone |
