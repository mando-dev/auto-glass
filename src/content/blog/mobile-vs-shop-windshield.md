Mobile windshield service works for the overwhelming majority of jobs. It's
worth knowing the handful of situations where a shop is genuinely the better
call, so you're not surprised either way.

## What mobile service actually needs

A mobile setup carries everything the job needs to it — the glass, the
resin or urethane, the tools, the power. What it needs from the location is
simpler than people expect:

- **Dry conditions.** Resin and urethane both need to cure without moisture
  interference, so active rain is the one weather condition that reliably
  postpones a mobile job.
- **Space to work.** Roughly a car width of clear room on one side is
  usually enough.
- **A reasonable temperature range.** Both resin and urethane cure within a
  specific range — most days in Orange County fall well inside it, but an
  unusually cold morning can occasionally push a job later in the day.

## When a shop is genuinely the better choice

- **Heavy or sustained rain.** Not every rainy day rules out mobile work,
  but a genuinely wet, exposed job site isn't the right place for fresh
  adhesive.
- **Certain calibration setups.** Some vehicles' camera calibration
  requires a controlled indoor space with specific target distances and
  lighting — this depends on the vehicle and the calibration method, and
  whoever does the work should be able to tell you whether yours needs it.
- **A damaged pinch weld.** The pinch weld is the metal channel the
  windshield bonds into. If it's rusted or damaged, that needs proper repair
  before new glass goes in, which is more of a shop job than a driveway one.

## The cure time question is the same either way

However you get it done, "safe drive-away time" — the point at which the
adhesive is cured enough to drive safely — depends on the specific urethane
used and the conditions it cured in, not on whether the work happened at a
shop or in your driveway. Whoever does the job should tell you the actual
number for your vehicle rather than a generic estimate.

## Cameras and calibration

Many vehicles from roughly 2018 onward mount a forward-facing camera to the
windshield for features like lane keeping or automatic emergency braking.
Replacing the glass on one of these vehicles can affect that camera's
reference point, and a calibration step may be part of the job. This applies
the same way whether the glass goes in at a shop or mobile — what matters is
asking directly, before the work starts, how it's handled for your specific
vehicle.

## The fleet angle

For a company running several vehicles, mobile service usually means one
visit to the yard rather than pulling each vehicle off the road separately.
Multiple vehicles get looked at in the same trip, parked wherever they
already sit.

## Preparing your parking spot

If you're booking mobile service, a few minutes of prep helps:

- Park somewhere level, out of direct exposure if rain is even a
  possibility that day
- Leave the car unlocked or have keys accessible at the appointment time
- Clear loose items from the dashboard near the windshield
